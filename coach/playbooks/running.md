# Playbook: Running

Sport-specific extensions for runners (5k / 10k / half / marathon / ultra).

## Session types

- **Easy / recovery** (Z1-2): conversational pace, aerobic base building. **The most important sessions in the plan.** ~70-80% of weekly volume.
- **Long run**: builds fatigue resistance and mitochondrial density. Weekly staple. 20-25% of weekly volume for standard marathon build.
- **Tempo / threshold** (Z3-Z4): 20-40 min sustained at half-marathon-ish pace. Builds lactate clearance.
- **Intervals** (Z4-Z5): 3-8 min repeats at 5k-10k pace. Builds VO2max and top-end.
- **Strides**: 6-8× 15-20s controlled acceleration to near-sprint. Neuromuscular. Add to end of easy runs.
- **Hill repeats**: 45-90s at threshold effort uphill, easy jog down. Strength + form + cardio.

## Distance-specific priorities

| Race | Long run | Weekly volume | Peak workout |
|------|----------|---------------|--------------|
| 5k | 60-80 min | 40-60 km | 5-6× 1000m @ 5k pace |
| 10k | 90 min | 50-70 km | 4-5× 2000m @ 10k pace |
| Half marathon | 2:00-2:15 | 60-90 km | 4× 20 min @ HM pace |
| Marathon | 2:45-3:30 | 70-110 km | 3× 5 km @ MP + 10 km @ MP |
| Ultra | 3-6 hours (some back-to-back) | 80-140 km | Long time on feet, not pace |

## Zone anchoring

Preferred: **HR from a lab test** or a benchmark race (5k or 10k). Pace/km is secondary — pace drifts with heat, elevation, fatigue; HR doesn't.

For a rough anchor from race times:
- Marathon pace ≈ Z3 upper end
- Half marathon pace ≈ Z4 (sub-threshold)
- 10k pace ≈ Z4 upper / Z5 lower
- 5k pace ≈ Z5

## Progression rules

- **Never increase weekly volume by more than 10%** week-on-week. Injuries almost always come from volume jumps.
- **3:1 loading** (3 weeks build, 1 week recovery). 2:1 if fatigued or returning from a break.
- **Long run cap** at ~30% of weekly volume. Beyond that, injury risk climbs sharply.
- Two consecutive hard days is a red flag. Space quality sessions with easy runs between.

## Cadence + form

- **Target cadence:** 170-185 spm at all easy paces, higher at threshold+. Low cadence + long stride = injury vector.
- **Stryd or watch-based cadence** gives real-time feedback. Optional but useful.
- **Foot strike** doesn't matter as much as popular running literature claims. Cadence + posture matter more.

## Injury prevention

- **Rest days matter more than fast days.** Minimum 1 full rest day per week.
- **Strength work 1-2x/week** — glutes, calves, single-leg stability. 20-30 min bodyweight is enough for most amateurs.
- **Shoe rotation** — 2-3 shoes with different drops/stack heights. Reduces repetitive strain.
- **Signals to back off:** persistent morning HR elevation (+5 bpm over baseline), poor sleep despite fatigue, declining pace at same HR, niggles that don't clear in 48h.

## Race-week (for a peak race)

- **R-14 to R-7:** peak week (highest volume of build).
- **R-7 to R-1:** taper. Volume drops 40-60%. Keep intensity but shorten.
- **R-3:** race-eve openers — 20 min easy + 4-6 strides.
- **R-1:** rest or 15-20 min shake-out.
- **Fuelling:** carb load starts R-3 (7-10g carb/kg/day). Race-morning breakfast 2.5-3h before start.

## Common mistakes

- **Running easy runs too hard.** If you can't hold a full conversation, it's not easy.
- **Chasing race-day pace in training.** Race pace is a *by-product* of the training, not the *goal* of the training.
- **Increasing volume + intensity in the same week.** Pick one.
- **Skipping strength work.** Runners who don't lift get injured. Simple as.
